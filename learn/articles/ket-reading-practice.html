KET Reading Practice: A Simple Method for Better Accuracy

KET reading questions reward careful reading more than fast guessing. A learner may know every word in a sentence and still choose the wrong answer because the question asks for a detail, a purpose or a comparison. This guide gives families and teachers a repeatable practice method that takes about 20 minutes.

Use this rule: answer from evidence in the text. First predict what kind of information you need, then underline the words that prove your choice.

1. Read the question before the passage

Look for the key words in the question. Is it asking who, where, when, why or how? A “where” question needs a place; a “why” question needs a reason. This small distinction prevents a common mistake: choosing an answer that is mentioned in the text but does not answer the question.

2. Match meaning, not only words

Cambridge tasks often paraphrase the passage. “The shop is not open on Monday” may become “You cannot buy anything there on Monday.” Teach yourself to connect meaning. Circle a word in the option that seems important, then find the sentence in the passage that expresses the same idea in different words.

3. Check every option

Do not stop at the first option that sounds possible. Cross out choices that are too broad, too narrow or unsupported. For a notice, ask what action the reader should take. For a short email, ask what the writer wants the friend to know. If an option adds information that the text never gives, leave it out.

A 20-minute practice routine

  1. Three minutes: read the title and questions; predict the information type.
  2. Eight minutes: read once for the main idea, then scan for evidence.
  3. Five minutes: choose answers and write one evidence phrase beside each.
  4. Four minutes: review mistakes. Record whether the problem was vocabulary, detail, inference or rushing.

Seven-day review plan

  • Day 1: notices and signs; identify the intended action.
  • Day 2: short messages; identify the writer’s purpose.
  • Day 3: matching descriptions; underline the shared meaning.
  • Day 4: a short factual text; make a three-line outline.
  • Day 5: repeat one task with a timer.
  • Day 6: review the error record and create five example sentences.
  • Day 7: complete a mixed set and explain two answers aloud.

Make the result useful

A score is only useful when it changes the next practice task. If most errors are details, slow down and mark evidence.
