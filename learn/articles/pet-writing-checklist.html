PET Writing Checklist: Improve a Short Email in Ten Minutes

A good PET email answers the task clearly before it tries to sound advanced. Learners often lose marks by forgetting one prompt, writing disconnected sentences or leaving basic errors unchecked. This ten-minute checklist gives each stage a clear purpose.

Remember the task: your reader should understand why you are writing, receive every piece of requested information and know what to do next.

Minute 1: identify the reader and purpose

Underline the situation and the three points in the prompt. Is the reader a friend, a teacher or a club organiser? A friendly email can use a relaxed opening, while a request to an organiser needs a more careful tone. Write one sentence that states the purpose before you begin.

Minutes 2–3: make a three-part plan

  1. Opening: greet the reader and explain the reason for writing.
  2. Middle: answer each prompt with a detail or example.
  3. Closing: ask a question, suggest a next step or say goodbye.

Planning prevents a common problem: writing a long first paragraph and then rushing the final prompt. Keep one short note for each required point.

Minutes 4–7: write connected sentences

Use simple linking words such as because, but, so, then and however. Add one specific detail to make the message natural. “I enjoyed the trip” is clear; “I enjoyed the trip because the guide showed us the old market” is more useful. Do not add details that make the message confusing or unrelated.

Minutes 8–10: check in four passes

  • Task: did you answer every prompt?
  • Organisation: can the reader follow the order?
  • Grammar: check verb tense, subject-verb agreement and countable nouns.
  • Presentation: check names, punctuation and the greeting and closing.

Build a personal correction list

Do not copy every correction into a large notebook. Keep five patterns that you repeatedly miss. For example, you might write “I went yesterday,” “She goes every day,” and “I have visited twice.” Review the pattern with new examples rather than memorising one corrected email.

What makes practice effective

Write one short email, check it with the list and rewrite only the sentences that need work. Then read the final version aloud. A ten-minute cycle done three times a week gives clearer progress than writing one long piece and never reviewing it.
