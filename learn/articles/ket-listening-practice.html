KET Listening Practice at Home: A Three-Pass Routine

KET listening can feel difficult because the recording moves on before a learner has finished translating. The solution is to train three separate skills: predicting the situation, catching the important detail and recovering after a missed word. This routine uses short audio and can be completed in 15 to 20 minutes.

Choose one short recording: use audio you are allowed to use, ideally between 30 seconds and two minutes. Repeating a small clip is more useful than collecting many clips and understanding none of them.

Before listening: make a prediction

Read the question and look at any picture or title. Ask what kind of answer you need: a number, a place, a person, a time or an action. Predict two or three possible words. Prediction gives your attention a target and makes the first listen less passive.

First listen: understand the situation

Listen once without pausing. Do not try to write every word. Decide who is speaking, where they are and what they want. If you miss one phrase, keep listening. The next sentence often gives enough context to recover the meaning.

Second listen: catch evidence

Listen again and write only the details that answer the question. Compare your notes with the answer choices. Watch for changes: a speaker may mention one time and then correct it, or say that a plan has moved to another place. The final detail is often the one that matters.

Third pass: turn sound into language

Use a transcript only after your own attempt. Circle one phrase that was hard to hear and say it aloud three times. Then explain the same idea in your own words. This last step connects listening with speaking and shows whether the learner understood the message or only guessed the answer.

A 20-minute home routine

  1. Two minutes: preview the topic and questions.
  2. Three minutes: listen once for the situation.
  3. Five minutes: listen again and record evidence.
  4. Five minutes: check the transcript and correct one misunderstanding.
  5. Five minutes: retell the message or answer one question aloud.

Keep an error record

After each practice, write one label: vocabulary, speed, detail, distraction or spelling. Review the same label for a week. If “speed” appears often, use shorter clips and repeat them. If “detail” appears often, practise numbers, names and changes in plan. A simple record tells you what to practise next.
